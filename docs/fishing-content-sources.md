# Fishing content sources (signature species)

Every Fishbook species has a real football fact with its source URL in the data (`club.source` / `club.sources`) and a short
"fish tale" (`story`). This page lists the sources for the **signature species** added from 6 Nov 2026 on
(`lib/town/fishing/shoreSignatures.ts`), one row per fact. The original 73 species keep their sources in the data files and in
the tables in `docs/fishing.md`; their stories (`lib/town/fishing/fishStories.ts`) only retell facts already sourced there.

Rules (owner, 6 Nov 2026): a fact is kept only when the page linked below states it; facts are written in our own words; a
country's dish, a place name, a port or a national fish is NOT a football fact. Every fact names a real football entity (club,
national team, stadium, mascot, player, tournament, chant or federation: `club.entity`, enforced by tests/fishing-signatures.cjs).
When no club, team, mascot or player could be tied to the animal, the fact is split and labelled **Fun fish fact** ("Fun fish
fact: … Football: …", shown as "Fun fish fact + real football fact") with a real fact about that place's club or team. When a
nickname names a boat or a family of animals rather than the exact species (Trawler Boys, Thoniers, the Kolkata prawn), the
species is a teaching choice and the fact never claims it. Kids see a plain "Source: Wikipedia" credit and no links. Which spot an animal lives at, and the playful half of each story,
are island fiction. All pages checked on 6 Nov 2026.

## Checked and cut (wave 1)

- **Concarneau "Les Thoniers"** (tuna): the club page does not give the nickname. Cut.
- **Mazatlán "shrimp capital"**: Mazatlán F.C.'s page says nothing about shrimp (nickname "Cañoneros"). Cut.
- **Holstein Kiel "Die Störche"**: marked "citation needed" on Wikipedia, so the fact does not use the nickname.
- **Fraserburgh "The Broch"**: first pass missed it; the club page (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraserburgh_F.C.) states it, so it is now shown. The first-pass fact cited only the town page for the Highland League title: source fixed.
- **FC Gifu (ayu, cormorant fishing) and Kataller Toyama (firefly squid)**: neither club page mentions the animal. Cut.
- **Southend United "Elvis the Eel" mascot**: not on the club page. Cut.
- **Salmon (Deportes Puerto Montt "Salmoneros")**: only an old 2009 RSSSF list; the club page could not be checked. Cut.
- **Clam chowder / New England Revolution**: the chowder page does not name the clam species. Cut.
- **Milkfish (Philippines)**: Wikipedia notes the "national fish" label has no basis in Philippine law. Cut.
- **"El Pulpo" goalkeepers (octopus)**: verifiable, but a second octopus is too close to Paul the Octopus; kept for a later wave.
- **Charlton "Addicks" (haddocks)** and **Worthing "Mackerel Men"**: real, but the haddock and mackerel are already in the Fishbook.
- **Perranporth "the Pilchards"**: the pilchard is the same species as the sardine already in the Fishbook.
- **Senegal's AFCON titles**: the page reports a disputed 2026 result, so the fact uses the 2002 World Cup instead.

## Second-pass review (6 Nov 2026): cut or re-linked

- **Ghost Shrimp / Cameroon ("Rio dos Camarões")**: a country named after shrimp is a place name, not football. Species replaced
  by the Giant River Prawn (Mohun Bagan fans' prawn, ESPN). Wave 1 is not pushed yet; a save holding `ghost-shrimp` simply drops it (sanitizeFishbook keeps FISH ids only).
- **Flying fish / Barbados national dish**: dish, not football; the Barbados FA crest shows a trident, not a fish. Re-linked to the
  Cocos (Keeling) Islands club Flying Fish (RSSSF 2003-04 participants list).
- **Albacore / Ecuador encebollado**: dish. Re-linked to US Concarneau "Les Thoniers" (French Wikipedia infobox; NW Sport).
- **Mud crab / Singapore chilli crab**: dish. Re-linked to Samut Prakan United "The Crab Devils" (club page).
- **Hilsa / Bangladesh national fish**: national fish. Re-linked to East Bengal fans' hilsa in the Kolkata derby (ESPN).
- **Labelled "Fun fish fact"** (no verifiable club, team, mascot or player tied to the animal; the football half is real and
  sourced): white grouper (Senegal), queen conch (Bahamas), cockle (Swansea City), Dover sole (Dover Athletic), pollock
  (Peterhead, plus the SPFL title page), langoustine (Fraserburgh), Greenland shark (Iceland at Euro 2016), galjoen (Bafana
  Bafana), queen scallop (F.C. Isle of Man), red scorpionfish (Stade Vélodrome), moray (Anguilla, FIFA), corvina (Peru's sash),
  pink cusk-eel (Chile, Copa América), sprat (Holstein Kiel). Searched and not found: a "Flying Fish", "Conch", "Cockle",
  scallop-crest, grouper, scorpionfish or galjoen nickname; J.League mascots (only Vanta the squid and FC Ryukyu's whale shark,
  already in the book); SD Compostela's crest (no scallop found).
- **Left for a later wave**: Colón de Santa Fe "Sabaleros" (the English page translates sábalo as tarpon, but the Paraná sábalo
  is a different freshwater fish, so it was not used).
