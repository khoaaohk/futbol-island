# Explore balls: seven hidden balls, seven new mini lessons (Oct 21 2026)

User: "add a ball to find in each of the explore areas. Make sure the mini lesson after each ball is collected for this area is new and not related or similar to any other ones."

Each Explore reef zone hides ONE football (`lib/town/water/diveBalls.ts`; drawn by `lib/graphics/diveBall.ts`; a `ball` find in `diveSpots.ts`). Swim close, tap **Pick up** (or E): the ball spins up into your bag, the card shows the lesson (a **Real football fact** with a plain-text credit), +5 coins once ever through the Training meter (`dive:<zone>:hidden-ball`), and the card's last line counts the balls ("Explore balls found: 3 of 7"). The ball is saved in the dive finds store (`fi2-dive-finds-v1`, about 20 bytes a zone). It is NOT a reef journey task: the buddy fish never leads to it and the reef's x/y count leaves it out, so it stays a secret. Analytics: `wa:dive_ball_find` (first find of each ball), on the Water admin tile.

## How the lessons were checked

1. **Inventory.** Every lesson, fact, story, tip and quiz a kid can meet was listed: ball hunt lessons (island, Summit, Coral Cay), the 51 bug stories, fish and sea legends, the dive finds, sparkle facts and reef card, museum exhibits and experiences, World Cup ball facts, townsfolk and shop lines, curriculum and coaching ideas, books, arcade and job tips, docs lesson tables (about 3,000 items).
2. **Topics.** For each candidate topic the codebase was searched (lib, components, app, docs). Rejected because the game already teaches them: first floodlit match (Bramall Lane), goal nets (Brodie), shin guards (Widdowson), vanishing spray, goal-line technology, the penalty kick (McCrum), pitch mowing stripes, altitude (La Paz), ball sizes, the dropped ball, scoring straight from the kick-off, drinks breaks, Dick, Kerr Ladies, blind / walking / amputee / powerchair football, the first radio commentary, a World Cup ball in space.
3. **Facts.** Each lesson was checked against the source below; anything that sources disagree on was left out (for example the year the Stirling ball was found).
4. **Test.** `tests/explore-balls.cjs` checks that each lesson's topic words appear nowhere else in the game's kid-facing code and data (lib, components, app) or the docs' lesson tables, that no 6-word phrase is shared with any other text, and that no two of the seven share a topic.

## The lesson texts (≤ 280 characters each)

- **The world’s oldest football** (Stirling Smith Art Gallery and Museum): The oldest football ever found was hidden behind wooden panels in Stirling Castle, Scotland. It was made around 1540 to 1570 from leather stitched round a pig’s bladder full of air. It has patches, so someone really played with it!
- **White posts, no rope** (The FA, Laws of the Game, Law 1): Goalposts and the crossbar must be white, and no thicker than 12 cm. If the crossbar breaks in a match, play stops until it is fixed. A rope is not allowed instead, and if the bar cannot be fixed, the match is called off.
- **The fourth official** (IFAB, Laws of the Game, Law 6): Big matches have a fourth official beside the team benches. Before kick-off they help check the match balls and the players’ equipment. During the game they help with substitutes, and in some competitions they take over if the referee gets hurt.
- **Lamps that grow grass** (Pitchcare): Seagrass needs sunlight, and so does a pitch. Where a stadium’s roof and stands shade the grass, groundstaff wheel huge lamp rigs over it, so the grass keeps growing even in the dark days of winter.
- **Cuju: a goal up high** (FIFA Museum): Over 2,000 years ago in China, people played cuju, a kicking game. In one version the goal was a net hung high between two tall poles, with a round hole in the middle, and players aimed to kick the ball through that hole.
- **The football city** (LUMS): Most of the world’s hand-stitched footballs come from one city: Sialkot in Pakistan. Skilled stitchers sew the panels together by hand, and the official ball of the 2026 World Cup was made in Sialkot too.
- **A picture made of fans** (Wikipedia, "Tifo"): A tifo is a giant picture made by fans in the stands. Thousands of people hold up coloured cards or flags at the same moment, and together they make one huge image, the way many small plates make one turtle shell.
