# 9v9 story production — September 19, 2026

Local, not deployed. These original stories support developing players, with language intended to be accessible around ages 10–12. The 9v9 path is an organizational choice, not an age classification.

## Story treatments

- **Different Tides:** three tide pools fill through different channels. The camera moves closer, channels connect, and a shared ball follows the water. Ari notices personal progress and chooses a shoulder-check target with a coach.
- **The Harbour at Night:** a straining sail folds into a resting boat, held by a harbour rope beneath a crescent moon; it reopens toward morning. Noor makes room for rest and talks to a trusted adult. This is supportive storytelling, without medical claims.
- **The Unfinished Map:** a blocked pencil route gives way to a curved path, a new bridge and a traveling ball. Mika asks for specific feedback and tries scanning the passing lane.

The shared player supplies grain, transition composition, highlighted labels, captions, controls and audio sequencing. Draw functions supply original responsive Canvas2D geometry. No raster assets, dependencies, timers, audio contexts or independent render loops are added. Canvas calls are bounded; shared dash arrays are allocated at module load. Reduced motion fixes the within-beat time/progress while preserving meaning at each beat change.

## Temporary narration

Voice: built-in macOS Samantha, `say -v Samantha -r 145`. This is temporary synthetic narration, not a human performance or cloned voice. AIFF intermediates stayed in `/tmp`; existing ffmpeg encoded mono AAC at 64 kbps into M4A. `afinfo` measured every installed file. Initial sandbox speech produced empty files; approved `say` execution generated valid audio. afconvert could not see an AAC encoder in the sandbox, so existing ffmpeg was used without escalation.

Durations below include encoded-audio padding. Each beat timeline includes a reflective pause after narration; pause length is adjusted across each story to approximately 60 seconds.
