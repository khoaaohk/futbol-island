## 0. Print setup

- **Paper** `#f0ece2`. **Inks (3 + navy):** purple `#765ba7`, orange `#ff6c2f`, yellow `#ffe800`, navy `#22366b` (last). Print order: yellow → orange → purple → navy. Overprints used on purpose: orange × yellow = deep orange (the striker stripe); purple × orange = rust (defender bands, opponent cloth pressing on mine); purple × yellow = olive (the pitch as cloth).
- **Background construction (unique to this story): "shirt fabric knit rows and a growing outline."** Every background is cloth: a grainy ink field (.5–.6) carrying **knit rows** — hand-cut chevron (V) rows 22 u apart printed as a darker tone of the same ink (.75 rows over a .5 field), rows that bend where the cloth hangs, pucker where it is pulled, compress when it shrinks. Over the cloth a **navy outline** of a shirt that grows across the story (w 360 → 520 → 900 u) and finally opens into a dashed line. Beyond the outline the sheet becomes **patchwork**: adjacent panels of other knits (cable = paired wavy lines; seed = dot rows; rib = vertical bars) in the three inks, each with a hand-cut edge and a **running-stitch seam** (navy dashes 16 u) joining it to its neighbour. This is jersey knit and patchwork — *not* a warp/weft lattice (that is Woven Court's) and not ribbons.
- **How it differs from the other 11v11 stories:** quiet-lantern = radial stepped light wedges in a dark room; boat-weather = weather fronts, rain streaks, sailcloth; regulate = nested arches, torn channel, diagonal ribbons + scribble disc (reserved); reset (legacy) = a bending branch in wind bands; loss (legacy) = settling water ripples. Here everything is stitched cloth: chevron rows, a numeral, seams, patches.
- **Lead imagery:** a shirt outline; a number (a hand-cut **stencil numeral shape**, authored as a path — not typeset text; if the art director rules it lettering, swap for a stripe count); fabric knit rows; the outline opening (dashed, growing); running stitch; a loose thread (the feeling, later the guide line). **Lead imagery not used by any other story in this path.**
- **Generic shapes:** no eye, no ear, no hand, no speech bubble. "Someone asks" = the collar's garment **tag** flips out; "notice before receiving" = the shirt outline turns (collar and numeral swing); "friends / people who know you" = patches stitched around mine carrying my colour.

## 2. Dominant material metaphor and its development

**The shirt as stitched cloth with an outline that is a line, not a wall.** Development: ch1 a hanging shirt whose numeral is re-printed three times (roles), then whose rows run out past the outline (more than the role) → ch2 the outline drawn alone while bands of knit flow straight through it; the game stitched in as a crest; other knits patch on outside (what you can become) → ch3 things change on the peg wall: numeral swapped, shirt folded on the far peg, a purple panel presses and a thread snags; the snag is contoured and kept; the quilt prints around it (whole value) → ch4 the pitch as olive cloth: the tag asks, the snagged thread becomes the guide line to the gap, ties to a teammate around an opponent, the shirt turns before the crest-ball arrives → ch5 the stitched marks unpick and travel to every position, then off the pitch into patches that carry my colour → ch6 the outline is squeezed, pushes back, opens into a dashed line and the rows flow through it (room to grow). Same cloth world at four scales (close on the back → outline as landscape → peg wall → pitch → quilt → outline again), the loose thread of ch3 returning as the route of ch4–5 and the numeral returning in ch6.

## 5. What would make this fail (Astra-version symptoms to avoid)

- A small centred shirt icon on a flat colour: the shirt fills the frame at ch1 and the outline exceeds the frame at ch2/ch6; every background is grainy knit with visible rows.
- A warp/weft grid (that is Woven Court) or ribbons/arches/channel (regulate): here chevron knit rows and patches only.
- A typeset number, a name, a caption or any lettering in the artwork; the numeral is an authored stencil shape and headlines are HTML.
- Speech bubbles for "asks", an eye for "notice", hands for "care": tag, turn and stitched marks instead.
- Unrelated symbols for "friends, interests": they are patches of other knits stitched to mine and carrying my row.
- Flat holds; a re-print without a misregistration settle; pressure that does not compress the rows; a seam that zooms out.

## 6. Delight (§4d)

- **Touch reaction `touch(sheet,x,y,age)` ≤ 0.8 s:** a **thread pull**: a navy thread (6 u) is plucked out of the knit at the tap point — it arcs 60 u out with overshoot (0.25 s), the three nearest rows pucker toward it, then it re-stitches itself back as four running-stitch dashes that tighten (0.55 s) and the rows relax. If the point is on the collar, the tag flips instead (0.4 s). Navy thread + row deformation only. Reduced motion: one static plucked loop for 0.4 s.
- **Secondary motion that reacts to main actions:** the hanging shirt's pendulum (restarted by every re-print, slide or press; decays 2.5 s); row pucker/compression (toward pulls, under presses, relaxing on "room"); loose stitch ends (a few dashes near a moving seam lag and snap tight); the tag (swings after flipping); torn patch edges (flap once when pushed); the crest's dashes (tighten when the ball arrives).
- **Headline arrival:** OUTLINE / WHOLE / ASK / ROOM pop in with the misregistration settle (CSS); none in ch1 and ch5.
- **End card:** the tag flips out once and settles; Replay stamp.
