## The one world

A **training pitch seen from the side at eye level**: a green pitch (yellow × blue) with paper lines runs back to a **navy torn hedge line** on the horizon; a small **goal** (posts, bar, net) stands far away on the horizon; a **wall of red paper cones** is lined up along the far side of the pitch; the near edge of the frame is a torn band of **hand-cut grass tufts** (darker green with paper blades). Above the hedge, cream sky with a faint yellow screen and a few **red arrow-fletching confetti** drifting. The metaphor object is an **ARCHERY TARGET**: big concentric paper rings — yellow centre, red ring, blue outer ring, navy contour rings — standing on a navy A-frame stand at the edge of the pitch. The **archer** is a blue cut-paper figure with a simple navy bow (a curved limb + a string); the **player** is a paper cut-out with a yellow shirt and navy limbs (room-to-invent's `figure()` pictogram, head + torso ≥ 45 % of the safe height when the subject); the **coach** is a bigger navy figure who points; the **ball** is a paper football with a navy hollow-pentagon seam net (no filled pentagons — distinct from the other 9v9 balls) and a warm yellow shade crescent. Arrows are navy shafts with red fletching; every arrow that lands gets a paper ring around it.

**How this differs from its neighbours (§1b).** different-tides owns tide-line bands on sand; harbour-night owns the night harbour; unfinished-map owns pencilled contours; grit owns underground strata. Nobody has a side-view training pitch with a hedge horizon, a cone wall and a grass-tuft foreground, and nobody has an archery target, a bow, arrows, a training clock, a card rail or a tally card. Concentric rings appear here **only as the target object** (the subject, never a background construction), and the pitch is not a court map (no centre circle). Reset/loss (11v11) are side-view pitches too, but with a tree / a puddle at dusk and purple/orange inks; this one is a daylight training ground in yellow/red/blue.

**Inks** (print order): yellow `#ffe800` → red `#ff665e` → blue `#0078bf` → navy `#22366b` on cream `#f0ece2`. **Role colours.** Pitch = yellow .8 × blue .45 (green) with navy .1 mown bands; hedge = navy .75; grass tufts = blue .6 over the yellow (darker green); the target = yellow centre / red ring / blue ring / navy contours on paper; the player = paper body, **yellow shirt**, navy limbs, blue head shade; the **weaker (left) foot** = a yellow boot on the player and a yellow boot mark on the card; the archer = blue figure (paper knocked out beneath); the coach = navy figure; pressure = **red** cones (the cone wall, the gate, the cone-defender); arrows = navy shaft + red fletching, paper ring on impact; sight line = paper dots with yellow; the ball = paper + navy seam net + yellow shade; sparks = yellow on paper.

**Background construction (unique): a daylight training ground — navy torn hedge on the horizon, foreshortened green pitch with mown bands and paper lines, a red cone wall along the far side, a torn hand-cut grass-tuft band across the near edge, red fletch confetti in a cream sky.** Every chapter is this one world from a different distance and angle of attention (wide with the clock · the archer's range · the card rail · the coach's drill · the big target · two players).

**Headlines** (HTML, ≤ 2 words, key points only; none may duplicate another story's word): ch1 none · ch2 **AIM** (at 5.05 "aims") · ch3 **PICK ONE** (at 3.4 "choose one thing" — different-tides already owns ONE THING in this path) · ch4 **MATCH IT** (at 1.86) · ch5 **ONCE MORE** (at 5.8 "Adjust, and try once more" — boat-weather already owns ADJUST) · ch6 none.

## Football truth check

ch1: an aimless kick rolls the ball 300 u and stops (no pass, no receiver — that is the point). ch2: no football. ch3: the ball stays at the player's foot while walking (dribble pace). ch4: passes are .17 s rolls over 360 u through a gate of two cones to a marker (a passing drill), the left/weaker foot is the marked front foot; the coach's pass is a .55 s ground pass over 500 u; the first touch takes the ball 170 u away from the pressure cone, and the player moves after it (touch away from pressure, then move). ch5: the heavy touch runs 250 u (too far), the pass behind travels past the target (wrong side); the corrected touch runs 120 u. ch6: the target player keeps the ball at the feet; the wanderer's kicks go nowhere.
