## 0. Print setup

- **Paper** `#f0ece2`. **Inks (3 + navy):** purple `#765ba7`, orange `#ff6c2f`, blue `#0078bf`, navy `#22366b` (last). Print order: orange → blue → purple → navy. - **Background construction (unique to this story): "weather fronts, rain streaks and sailcloth."** (1) **Fronts**: one huge grainy orange field per weather beat with a single hand-torn *leading edge* (teeth 40–120 u) that advances across the sheet under a gust envelope — a front is one field with one moving torn edge (not a channel between two fields). (2) **Rain streaks**: diagonal halftone streaks (blue, 1200 seeds, 60–200 u long, angle −30…−40°) whose density is a weather variable, thinning inside "room" knockouts. (3) **Sailcloth**: paper with 5–6 navy seam lines parallel to the leech, wobble, and the sky's ink faintly showing *through* the cloth (translucency .15–.3) — the sail is a print you can see the weather through. Water: blue grainy field .5–.55 with **paper foam crests** (short hand-cut arcs 40–200 u drifting with the sea), never ripple rings and never horizontal bands.
- **How it differs from the other 11v11 stories:** quiet-lantern = radial stepped light wedges in a dark room; more-shirt = knit rows and a growing outline; regulate = nested arches, torn channel, diagonal ribbons + scribble disc (reserved); reset (legacy) = a bending branch in wind bands; loss (legacy) = settling water ripples. - **Lead imagery:** sail, wind (orange streak halftone under gust envelopes), rain fronts, rudder/tiller, a heading line (navy dashed line laid on the water). **Lead imagery not used by any other story in this path.** Cross-path note: harbour-night (9v9) has a moored, folded sail and a lashed cargo buoy in still water; here the sail is the protagonist under gust envelopes (backwinds, fills, tacks) with a tiller and heading line, the buoy is free on open crests, and no harbour, moon or stars appear until the two posts of ch6.

## 2. Dominant material metaphor and its development

**Sailcloth against weather.** The weather is the game's uncontrollable force (a front's torn edge + rain + gusts with onset-peak-recovery); the sail is the player's response — cloth you can see the weather through, that only works when it is *set* to the wind; the tiller/heading line is the one clear choice. Development: ch1 the sea throws the buoy, an opponent hull cuts in, a decision flag snaps up; the tiller swings free → ch2 the wind shifts and backwinds the untrimmed sail (you cannot command it), the sheet is hauled and the sail fills; a pennant asks for help and is answered → ch3 a squall slams in before anyone can act; the feeling gets a contour; the cloth exhales and inhales and a clear patch opens with a fork → ch4 sea-lanes: recover to the ring, the pennant looks astern, one solid line and the buoy passed along it → ch5 a crest moves the boat off its line (influence, not control); flags talk; two shadows overlap into indigo covered water; the shore mast answers → ch6 heavier rain, the cloth shows the weather through it, a tack, a tow-line to the purple sail, both heading for the harbour mouth. Same world at four scales: deck (1,3,6), mast (2), sea-lanes from above (4), two boats at sea level (5); the heading line appears in every chapter from ch1's hint to ch6's solid line.

## 5. What would make this fail (Astra-version symptoms to avoid)

- A flat blue background with a boat icon in the middle: the water must be a grainy blue field with paper crests, the sky a front with a torn edge, and the sail must fill ≥ 60 % of the safe height when it is the subject.
- Rain as a static overlay on a still scene: every gust is an envelope that bends the sail, heels the hull, throws spray and drags the camera, then recovers.
- Speech bubbles for "talk" or an eye for "check your shoulder": here flags talk and the pennant/camera do the looking.
- Ripple rings on the water (that is loss / signal-water) or horizontal wave bands; only crests.
- A goal frame / boots / a pitch with icons: the pitch is sea-lanes, the destination is a harbour mouth, the ball is a buoy and appears only twice.
- Holding a print while the narration continues; the fork/heading line not changing at "one clear action"; a seam that zooms out.
- Lettering in the artwork; headlines are HTML and at most two words.
