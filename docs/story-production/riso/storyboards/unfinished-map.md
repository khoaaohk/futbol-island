## Lead imagery (owned by this story)

**A pencilled map**: **contour lines** (wobbly closed pencil loops stepping up two hills), a **faint pencil grid**, a **river band**, an **unfinished coastline** (the contour simply stops, with construction ticks), **the pencil** itself (navy stick with a cone tip and a red eraser end) that draws, taps, flips and rubs, **eraser crumbs**, **margin-note frames** (pencilled boxes holding drawn marks, never letters), **landmarks** (a **bridge** of three strokes, a **cairn**, **flags**), a **route being redrawn** (dashed pencil → yellow lane), a **red verdict stamp** that tries to print over the map, and the **ball as a map marker** (paper disc, navy panels, a pin). ## Background construction (unique to this story): pencilled contours and a faint grid on cream paper

Every chapter is printed on **cream paper that stays mostly visible**: a **faint pencil grid** (navy .12 lines every 120 u, wobbly, seeded), **contour lines** (navy .5 pencil loops 60 u apart stepping up each hill, pressure-varied, with gaps), **stepped teal terrain tone** between the inner contours (teal .12 / .22 / .32 — stepped tone as elevation, never as arches), the **river band** (teal .35, torn edges, grain inside), and the **unfinished coastline** whose contour stops with three construction ticks. Grain lives inside every tone band and inside the pencil's own line (a speckled graphite edge). Confetti accents = **eraser crumbs** (paper knockouts with navy flecks) and **pencil shavings** (small teal/navy curls). The huge-motif slot is the **ear-hill** (ch1), the **bridge** (ch2/6) and the **marker** (ch5).

How it differs from the other 9v9 stories: different-tides is wet sand in stepped horizontal tide bands; harbour-night is a dark overprint night field with a stepped lantern glow; grit is deep underground strata giving way to sky. The Unfinished Map is the only one where the paper is the ground and the ink is a pencil's line: contours, grid, crumbs.

## Dominant material metaphor and how it develops

**A pencilled map that is still being drawn.** Feedback is a new pencilled detail; a verdict is a red stamp that cannot print on paper that is still being worked on. Development:

1. a mistake is tapped on the route; the advice reaches the ear-hill; a red stamp drops and dents the paper; the marker is held by the contours →
2. the full map: the route is redrawn to the X, the stamp residue erased, a bridge added, a yellow lane chosen over the bridge →
3. the margin: the coach's chevron; the empty question frame; the camera looks for the opponent then the teammate; flags become landmarks; the lane goes around →
4. close: the defender-marker slides into the lane and buckles it; the teammate offers a new angle; the pass goes the other way; the angle is noted →
5. the marker huge: the pass's information as a note beside it; the stamp fails to print on the marker and slides off; a gentler contour is drawn around it →
6. the map later: the bridge kept, a question frame at the unfinished coast answered with a new contour, the marker rolls on, the route continues past the edge.

Later chapters revisit earlier places at another scale: ch6 is ch2's map after two more sessions; ch5's margin note is the frame from ch3; ch4's lane is the yellow lane drawn in ch2.

## Inks and role colours

Triple: **teal `#00838a`, yellow `#ffe800`, bright red `#ff665e`** + navy `#22366b` on paper `#f0ece2`. Print order yellow → teal → red → navy; registration 1.6 px (a pencil map is nearly in register) re-seeded per chapter.
