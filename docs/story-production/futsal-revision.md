The chalk, weave, and kite are brief visual analogies supporting confidence, shared responsibility, and adaptation. Each concept has six narration beats, a question-led opening, a futsal application, and a practical closing cue. ## Shared image-generation direction

Generate a cinematic storyboard contact sheet, six equal landscape frames in a clean 3-column by 2-row grid. Each frame is a full-bleed film composition, not a slide, card, dashboard, or infographic. Sophisticated abstract motion-design artwork suitable for all ages. Saturated vermilion orange #F56732, electric cobalt #254BEA, warm cream #FFF1CF, vivid pink #EF8DB5, deep navy #112847. Dense tactile screen-print grain, dry chalk edges, layered cut-paper shapes, slight ink misregistration, bold negative space. Monumental cropped forms extend beyond every edge; different camera scales create cinematic rhythm. Only two or three dominant colors per frame. No people, faces, mascots, literal football pitches, tactical diagrams, arrows, balls, explanatory text, panel labels, typography, UI, or watermarks. Keep the bottom fifth less busy for captions later. This is one connected animation world: the main shape of each frame becomes the visual material of the next. Each individual frame must also survive a central portrait crop.

## The Chalk Line — creative confidence

Concept: action and feedback can build confidence; certainty is not a prerequisite. Visual vocabulary: chalk incision, narrow opening, expanding ribbon, imperfect loop, branching surface. Avoid literal chalkboard or hand drawing.

1. **Before you feel ready.** Extreme close-up of a huge cobalt circular mass cropped beyond three edges against orange. A hairline cream opening near its center is almost closed. Powdery edges and dense print grain. Transition: camera pushes into that small opening until it fills the screen.
2. **Room to explore.** Inside the opening, a luminous cream chalk stroke sweeps diagonally through a navy field. Its irregular edge sheds a few large pink flecks. It ends in an unfinished arc, deliberately open. Transition: follow the moving tip; the thin stroke thickens into a broad cream ribbon.
3. **Try something useful.** A monumental cream ribbon loops across electric cobalt, cropped by both side edges. One pink segment turns away from the expected curve, leaving an orange sliver visible beneath. Transition: camera rotates only slightly as the sliver opens into a wide gap.
4. **Notice your options.** Three broad curved bands occupy orange space: navy pressing in from above, cream bending through center, cobalt opening below. The bands make alternatives visible without arrows or route diagrams. Transition: push through the cream curve as it brushes the navy edge.
5. **A mistake gives information.** Macro view of the cream stroke interrupted by a rough pink overprint. A second offset cream stroke curves around the interruption; the earlier mark remains visible underneath. No red failure symbols or crosses. Transition: zoom outward; imperfect overlapping strokes reveal a larger expanding spiral.
6. **Look. Try. Learn.** The spiral opens into a confident, off-center cream fan of broad curves over orange; pink and cobalt inner layers remain uneven and visible. Large shapes fill the entire frame. Leave a calm central pocket for the concluding phrase added in production. Transition/end: settle on the open form without closing it into perfection.

Image prompt: Apply Shared image-generation direction above to these six exact visual frames in reading order. The sequence should feel like moving inside one chalk mark, not six drawings of a chalk mark. The emotional progression is hesitation, permission, experiment, attention, feedback, renewed willingness.

## The Woven Court — trust and shared responsibility

Concept: helping and being helped distribute responsibility; each person notices a useful job. Visual vocabulary: stretched ribbon, interlocking broad bands, shifting tension, repaired gap. No literal court, loom, hands, net, or player diagram.

1. **Share the weight.** A single broad cream ribbon is stretched across a navy frame, narrowed at its center by a large orange mass resting just above it. Shapes are huge, sculptural, and cropped. Transition: move closer to the narrow section; its paper fibers become the edges of new ribbons.
2. **Strength through connection.** Four immense cobalt, pink, orange, and cream ribbons cross over and under one another against navy. Intersections have tactile shadows and slight roughness; no regular grid. Transition: pass underneath one crossing so it fills the camera like an arch.
3. **Make help visible.** Under that arch, a pink band enters from the left to support a cream band bending from above; electric cobalt fills the newly opened negative space. Only three decisive masses. Transition: track the shared curve upward and rotate the composition a little.
4. **Notice the next job.** The same three bands shift their overlaps: one rises while another widens beneath it. The image communicates exchanging load through relative thickness and contact, not symbols or labels. Orange now dominates the full frame. Transition: an expanding navy seam leads into a gap.
5. **Respond together.** A large navy gap divides an irregular pink-and-orange weave. Broad cream and cobalt bands approach from opposite edges and overlap across it. The repair remains visible rather than erased. Transition: zoom out from the repaired seam until all crossing surfaces feel like one connected fabric.
6. **Offer. Listen. Support.** A bold, flowing woven surface fills the screen diagonally. Cream, pink, and cobalt bands remain individually recognizable while sharing a smooth overall curve. Navy negative space and orange grain retain depth. No perfect checkerboard. Transition/end: tension softens into a broad supported arc.

Image prompt: Apply Shared image-generation direction above to these six exact visual frames in reading order. Treat the weave as a cinematic abstract landscape of monumental colored bands. The emotional progression is overload, connection, visible help, shared work, repair, reliable support.

## The Kite That Turned — adapting when plans change

Concept: keep a useful purpose while changing the action to fit new information. Visual vocabulary: directional wedge, compressed folds, rotating diamond fragments, opening space, flexible curved bands. No literal sky, flying kite, string, clouds, or wind arrows.

1. **When the plan changes.** A huge cream wedge enters cobalt space from the lower left, aimed into a narrowing gap between two navy blocks. A vivid orange edge reveals pressure at the gap. Transition: follow the wedge into the narrowing space until its flat surfaces fill the frame.
2. **Adjust your angle.** The cream wedge folds into two offset diamond-like planes, one pink, turning across an orange field. A broad cobalt curve pushes diagonally behind them. No outline or literal kite detail. Transition: one rotating plane expands into a full-screen cream surface.
3. **Make room to notice.** A quiet cream field opens between a navy crescent and a pink curved edge. The previous visual compression gives way to generous negative space; a small cobalt facet preserves continuity. Transition: camera slowly backs out so the quiet gap becomes a visible opening.
4. **Keep more options open.** A wide orange band forks around an enormous navy curved mass, revealing cream space along the side. The cobalt facet travels visually into the side opening by overlap, not an arrow. Transition: follow the curved edge sideways rather than pushing forward.
5. **Move after the pass.** Pink and cobalt folded bands arc around the navy form from opposite edges, creating a new cream opening in the upper right. The same forms now face a different direction; preserve the earlier orange band underneath. Transition: zoom through this opening as the folded planes flatten into curves.
6. **Notice. Choose. Adjust.** Full-bleed orange with a monumental cream curve rising diagonally; cobalt and pink faceted layers turn with it. Navy anchors one corner. The final form is open, flexible, and clearly related to the original wedge. Transition/end: settle into a new orientation with space ahead.

Show adaptation through changing orientation and opening negative space, not through a narrative about a kite. The emotional progression is blocked expectation, flexibility, pause, noticing alternatives, coordinated movement, renewed direction.
