# World Cup Balls: Exact-Source Audit (3D models, photos, flat artwork, licences)

Researched 2026-10-05 for the Futbol Island museum gallery (`world-cup-balls.json`, 40 balls). The goal is to find sources that reproduce each real ball **exactly**, including the printed adidas/FIFA marks and the ball-name wordmarks, and to record what each source's licence allows.

## Method and limits (read first)

- **Sketchfab**: every model listed was checked through the public Sketchfab Data API (`/v3/search`, `/v3/models/{uid}`), which gave the licence, whether it can be downloaded, the price, the face count and the texture size. Accuracy ratings come from looking at each model's own Sketchfab thumbnail next to known photos of the real ball. That is a **thumbnail-level** check. Nobody has checked these models panel by panel; that only becomes possible after download.
- **Wikimedia Commons**: I crawled `Category:FIFA World Cup balls` two levels deep through the API (one request every 4 s or more, no 429s). For every file I recorded the licence, author and pixel size. For Azteca and Telstar I also looked at thumbnails to judge the angles.
- **CGTrader and TurboSquid** block automated access (HTTP 202 with an empty body, and 403). Listings for those sites come from search-engine results only, so **their price, licence and accuracy are unverified**.
- **Sketchfab prices** come from the API in US cents (`500` = $5.00). Sketchfab Store prices can change.
- "View-only" means the model is on Sketchfab with **no licence and no download**, so all rights are reserved. Using it needs the author's written permission.
- Nothing was downloaded except small thumbnails, kept in the scratchpad. Nothing was bought, no accounts were created, and no personal details were sent in any request.

### Licence key

| Label | Meaning (for the modeller's or photographer's own rights only) |
|---|---|
| **CC BY 4.0 / 3.0 / 2.0** | Commercial use and modification are allowed. You must give credit: title, author, source link, licence link, and a note if you changed it. |
| **CC BY-SA** | Same as CC BY, but anything you adapt from it (for example a texture built from the photo) must also be released under CC BY-SA. |
| **CC BY-NC** | Non-commercial use only. |
| **CC0 / Public domain** | No conditions (for "public domain", this is the uploader's own claim). |
| **Sketchfab Standard** | Paid licence. Commercial use in derivative works is allowed; you may not pass the asset on by itself. |
| **Sketchfab Editorial** | Paid licence. **No commercial or promotional use**, and nothing that suggests a link to a brand. Allowed only in works that comment on news or public-interest events (news, reviews, documentaries). It is meant for assets that contain third-party trademarks and **grants no trademark rights**. |
| **View-only (no licence)** | All rights reserved. No download. Needs the author's permission. |
| **ARR (all rights reserved)** | Copyright held, with no licence offered. "Contact for permission." |

**Important:** no model or photo licence covers **adidas's or FIFA's own rights** in the logos, wordmarks and printed artwork on the ball. See the licensing section at the end.

### Women's World Cup exact rebuild (Oct 6 2026)

All ten Women's World Cup balls now carry their print as six cube-face WebP decals (`public/museum/wcballs/decals/wwc-*.webp`, together covering the sphere). The build scripts and solved photo poses are in `scripts/wwc-balls/`.

**How the decals were made:**
- Each reference photo was registered to the ball's panel geometry (Kabsch fit on identified features, then refined on the image).
- The print was redrawn in the ball's measured inks: from the photo that sees each point most face-on, or folded over the print's measured symmetry where no photo sees it.
- Logos were redrawn from Commons SVGs where they exist, and otherwise cut or traced from the photos.

**Licences:**
- Decals derived from CC BY-SA photos are CC BY-SA, with the version given in each decal's `credit`.
- Decals traced only from all-rights-reserved reference photos (1999 Icon, 2003 Fevernova, 2007 Teamgeist Blue, and the inks and wordmark of the Oceaunz Final Pro) contain no photo pixels. They still reproduce adidas's artwork, so the photographers' rights risk is low but not zero.
- The FIFA emblems (including the en.wikipedia non-free SVGs used for the 2015 and 2019 emblems) and the adidas marks are trademarks. See the licensing section below.

### The bigger issue: adidas and FIFA rights, which no model or photo licence covers

- **What adidas holds.** The real balls carry adidas trademarks: the trefoil, the three-bar "Badge of Sport" and three stripes, and the registered ball names (Telstar, Tango, Azteca, Questra, Tricolore, Fevernova, Teamgeist, Jabulani, Brazuca, Al Rihla and Trionda). For example, Telstar is US Reg. 3508598 and Trionda is US Reg. 8225622. adidas also holds **copyright in the surface artwork** of the modern balls: Tango triads, Azteca and Etrusco motifs, Brazuca bands, Trionda waves, and so on.
- **What FIFA holds.** The balls also carry FIFA marks: the World Cup emblems (for example "FIFA WORLD CUP Qatar 2022", the 2006 and 2010 logos and the 2026 trophy emblem) and "Official Match Ball". FIFA enforces its marks hard.
- **Older third-party marks.** Pre-1970 balls carry other names, e.g. Slazenger, still a live mark owned by Frasers Group, and "Top Star". The 1930 balls and the 1966 final ball carry no marks.
- **What the CC licence on a model or photo actually covers.** It covers only that **modeller's or photographer's** own rights. It is not a licence from adidas or FIFA. Sketchfab's CC terms and Commons' trademark template both say this. Showing a photo on Commons for information is not the same as reproducing the artwork as a texture inside a product.
- **Risk for this app.** Copying the marks and artwork exactly onto 3D balls is **reproduction and trademark use**, not just naming the balls. Several points lower the risk without removing it: the app is educational, non-commercial and historical, it would carry a clear "not affiliated with or endorsed by adidas or FIFA" disclaimer, and it would sell nothing. Because the audience is children, a brand owner is more likely to object than to sue, but takedown risk is real, especially for the FIFA emblems. A fair-use argument (US) is uncertain, because exact replicas are not transformative.
- **Safest path to "exact".**
  1. Ask **adidas** (brand licensing / football PR) and **FIFA** (FIFA Museum / brand protection) for written permission for non-commercial educational display. A museum context is a strong request.
  2. While waiting, ship the CC BY models with credits.
  3. Keep a fallback "unbranded" texture for each ball, so any mark can be removed quickly if asked.
- **Attribution checklist for the app.**
  - Keep a `CREDITS` entry for every model and photo used: title, author, URL, licence, and whether it was modified.
  - Add a trademark notice, for example: "adidas, the trefoil, the 3-Stripes and ball names are trademarks of adidas AG. FIFA World Cup emblems are trademarks of FIFA. Futbol Island is not affiliated with or endorsed by adidas or FIFA."
  - Release BY-SA-derived textures as BY-SA, for example with a note beside the texture files.
