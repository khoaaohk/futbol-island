# Four more hardship pop-up books and their vending machines — September 29, 2026 (local, not deployed)

Four new true stories of footballers overcoming adversity, each sold by its own new vending machine. They use the same engine,
format, price (100 coins) and narration pipeline as the other books (`docs/player-books-research.md`, `docs/player-books/SPREADS.md`).

## Choosing the players

The 33 existing books (island + 32 players) already cover Messi, Falcão, Marta, Maldini, Zidane, Ronaldinho, Pelé, Cruyff,
Modrić, Cristiano Ronaldo, Iniesta, Vardy, Salah, Kane, Putellas, Bronze, Kerr, Buffon, Eriksen, De Bruyne, Ibrahimović,
Lukaku, Rashford, Ronaldo Nazário, Garrincha, Davies, Eusébio, Weah, Drogba, Mané, Saka and Hegerberg. From the suggested
list, Davies, Modrić, Salah, Vardy, Kerr, Eriksen and Buffon were therefore out.

| Book | Player | Game | Continent / era | Adversity | Football lesson | Machine |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cafu` | Cafu | men's | South America, 1980s–2002 | poverty; rejected by five clubs' youth teams | the overlapping full-back; being ready as a substitute | North Beach |

| `oshoala` | Asisat Oshoala | women's | Africa → Europe, 2000s–2021 | family and cultural resistance to girls playing; injury | timing a striker's run; discipline and respect; coming back from injury | Sharks Beach |

Candidates considered and not used: Son Heung-min (well sourced, but the adversity is a strict training upbringing rather than
hardship), Megan Rapinoe/Alex Morgan (strong equal-pay story, but less of a personal hardship story for this series), Ji So-yun
and Sam Kerr-style injury stories (Kerr already has a book), Eden Hazard (weak hardship angle).

**Deliberately left out:** the widely repeated story that Kanté collected rubbish as a boy. Kanté has never confirmed it, and
Africa Check rated it unproven (https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/meta-programme-fact-checks/ngolo-kante-went-garbage-collector-fifa-world-cup-winner-no).
Nadia Nadim's father's death is told only as "he was killed in the war"; no details. Her later controversies and her mother's
death in 2022 are outside the story's scope (it ends with her qualifying as a doctor).

Each book has six pages; every page has a year, title, short passage, a paper action (prompt + response, steps where the action
has several taps), a "Take it to your game" lesson and a source index. Coaching lessons are ours, not quotations. Attributed
paraphrases are marked "she says"/"he says" and come from the players' own interviews.

## Cafu — "Told no, again and again" (`lib/books/stories/cafu.ts`)

1. **Football in Jardim Irene** (São Paulo, 1970s): one of six children in a poor district; street football; nickname from the winger Cafuringa; football school at seven and futsal. *Lesson:* street games and futsal teach quick feet.
2. **No, no, no, no, no** (1980s): rejected by the youth teams of Corinthians, Palmeiras, Santos, Atlético Mineiro and Portuguesa. Action: flip the five cards. *Lesson:* one coach's no is not the end of your story.
3. **At last, a yes** (1988): São Paulo's youth team; Copa São Paulo youth tournament that year. *Lesson:* keep working as hard once you get your chance.
4. **A brand-new position** (1990s): Telê Santana moved him from right midfield to right-back; Copa Libertadores 1992 and 1993. Action: run the overlap. *Lesson:* try new positions; overlap outside your winger.
5. **Three finals in a row** (1994, 1998): came off the bench in the 1994 final after a teammate's injury; lost the 1998 final to France. *Lesson:* substitutes matter.
6. **Captain of Brazil** (2002): captain; beat Germany 2–0; "100% Jardim Irene" on his shirt; later a foundation in Jardim Irene; "everything I have, I owe to Jardim Irene". *Lesson:* remember where you started and help the players after you.

Sources: Wikipedia, Cafu (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafu) — rejections, São Paulo 1988, Telê Santana, 1994 substitution, three consecutive finals, 2002 captaincy and the shirt; CNN, "Cafu: The double World Cup winner" (Human to Hero, 2014, https://edition.cnn.com/2014/05/07/sport/cafu-human-to-hero) — Jardim Irene, street football, trials before São Paulo, conversion to right-back, the foundation and his quote.

## Nadia Nadim — "A long road to a new home" (`lib/books/stories/nadim.ts`)

1. **A girl from Herat** (1988–2000): born in Herat; her father, an army general, was killed in the war; her mother Hamida took the five girls away to stay safe. Action: pack the suitcase. *Lesson:* stay close to the people who look after you, and to your team.
2. **The long journey** (2000): Pakistan, a flight to Italy, hidden in a truck heading north; they hoped for the United Kingdom but reached Denmark. *Lesson:* good players adapt to the game in front of them.
3. **The pitch next door** (Aalborg, 2000): a refugee centre next to a football club where she saw girls playing; training at the centre; a local club at twelve. Action: three taps. *Lesson:* watch good players, then practise what you saw.
4. **Waiting to play for Denmark** (2008–2009): citizenship in 2008; FIFA's residency rule; the Danish FA's appeal and FIFA's exception; debut at the 2009 Algarve Cup; first naturalised Dane in a Denmark senior national team; more than 100 caps. *Lesson:* keep training while you wait.
5. **The penalty in the final** (Euro 2017): the equaliser in the 2–1 quarter-final win over Germany; a penalty in the final; lost 4–2 to the Netherlands. *Lesson:* a penalty routine.
6. **Doctor Nadia** (2022): studied medicine at Aarhus University while playing; qualified in January 2022; many languages; UNESCO Champion for Girls' and Women's Education (2019); "all women should have the same access and opportunities". *Lesson:* you can be more than one thing.

Sources: Wikipedia, Nadia Nadim (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Nadim) — Herat, father, eligibility ruling, 2009 debut, Euro 2017, 100th cap, medicine, UNESCO; UNRIC (UN Regional Information Centre), "Nadia Nadim: All women should have the same access and opportunities" (https://unric.org/en/nadia-nadim-all-women-should-have-the-same-access-and-opportunities/) — the refugee centre near Aalborg next to a football club where she saw girls playing; Arab News, "The remarkable story of Danish-Afghan Manchester City striker Nadia Nadim" (https://www.arabnews.com/node/1314476/amp) — Pakistan, Italy, the truck, reaching Denmark instead of the UK; Olympics.com (https://www.olympics.com/en/news/euro-2022-women-denmark-nadia-nadim-doctor) — studying while playing, qualifying as a doctor.

## N'Golo Kanté — "Told no, and still working" (`lib/books/stories/kante.ts`)

1. **A small club near Paris** (1999): born in Paris in 1991 to parents from Mali; JS Suresnes from eight for about ten years; his father died when he was eleven. *Lesson:* your club can be a second family.
2. **The answer was always no** (ages 12–16): academy trials at 12, 14 and 16, always rejected ("we already have players like him, or better"); his coach said he was small and didn't show off. Action: turn over the three cards. *Lesson:* when you hear no, ask what you can improve.
3. **Six divisions from the top** (Boulogne, 2010–2012): second team at 19, six divisions below the top; studied accountancy in case football did not work out; trained with the first team at 21; debut in the last 11 minutes of the 2011–12 season. *Lesson:* one step at a time; learn by watching better players.
4. **Winning the ball back** (Caen, 2013–2015): all 38 league games and promotion; then more ball recoveries than any player in Europe. Action: win the ball back. *Lesson:* read the passer's eyes and hips, step in early.
5. **Champions, and champions again** (2016, 2017): Leicester's surprise title with the most tackles and interceptions in the league; Chelsea's title; voted by the players the best in England. *Lesson:* win it, then give it quickly to a teammate.
6. **World champion** (2018): all seven matches, 4–2 final win over Croatia; "success in football is something collective". *Lesson:* play for your team.

Sources: Chelsea FC, "'The answer was always the same: no' — Kanté on dealing with rejection" (Kanté's own account, 2021, https://www.chelseafc.com/en/news/article/-the-answer-was-always-the-same--no----kante-on-dealing-with-rej) — trials at 12/14/16, Suresnes 10–19, Boulogne second team at 19 in the sixth division, accountancy, first-team training at 21, "success in football is something collective"; Wikipedia, N'Golo Kanté (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%27Golo_Kant%C3%A9) — birth, Malian parents, father's death at 11, coach Pierre Ville's view, 2012 debut, Caen, Leicester and Chelsea titles and stats, PFA Players' Player of the Year, 2018 World Cup.

## Asisat Oshoala — "Playing when few believed" (`lib/books/stories/oshoala.ts`)

1. **A girl who loved football** (Nigeria, 2000s): born in 1994 in Ikorodu, Lagos State; big family; girls weren't expected to play; parents didn't want her to; she hid where she was going; never wore a shirt with a woman's name on it. *Lesson:* make room for any player who wants to join in.
2. **A grandmother who believed**: trouble at home for playing; her grandmother's advice: be a good and nice person, respectful, disciplined. Action: three flaps. *Lesson:* respect and discipline make you a player every coach wants.
3. **The best player in Canada** (2014): FC Robo and Rivers Angels; U-20 Women's World Cup top scorer (7) and best player, Nigeria second; African title with the seniors. *Lesson:* time your run to meet the ball at speed.
4. **Her parents became believers** (2014): the talk with her parents; now they call her and know her game. *Lesson:* explain what football means to you, calmly and kindly.
5. **A long way from home** (2015–2021): first African player in the WSL (Liverpool), a knee injury and two months out; Barcelona; first African to score in a Women's Champions League final (2019); first African woman to win it (2021). *Lesson:* rest, rehab and come back step by step.
6. **Helping the next girls** (2019): six-time African Women's Footballer of the Year; people in Nigeria wear her Barcelona shirt; her foundation and girls' tournament in Lagos; "always remember where you come from". *Lesson:* help others get better.

Sources: CNN, "Asisat Oshoala: How a grandmother's belief gave birth to an African soccer superstar" (2021, https://www.cnn.com/2021/10/05/football/asisat-oshoala-football-barcelona-nigeria-womens-champions-league-cmd-spt-intl/index.html) — Ikorodu, family, gender roles, hiding, her grandmother's words, the talk with her parents, the jersey quote, "remember where you come from"; Wikipedia, Asisat Oshoala (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asisat_Oshoala) — clubs, 2014 U-20 World Cup, Liverpool injury, Champions League firsts, six awards, foundation; FIFA, "Get to know Asisat Oshoala" (https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/womens/womensworldcup/australia-new-zealand2023/articles/get-to-know-asisat-oshoala-achievements-success-nigeria-womens-world-cup-2023).

## Narration

Coach Bella (`kokoro_af_bella`, Kokoro-82M, speed 1.0) via `node scripts/build-book-narration.cjs <id>` with
`KOKORO_PYTHON` pointing at a recreated Python 3.12 Kokoro 0.9.4 venv (the old session venv was gone; the cached
`hexgrad/Kokoro-82M` model was reused offline). 24 tracks, 27.0–42.3 s, with sentence cues in
`public/voice/books/<id>/narration.json`. Thirteen new name pronunciations were appended to `scripts/plays/pronunciations.json`
(Nadim, Hamida, Algarve, Suresnes, Ikorodu, Cafuringa, Jardim, Irene, Palmeiras, Mineiro, Portuguesa, Telê, Santana); existing
entries (Cafu, Kanté, N'Golo, Oshoala, Asisat, Paulo, Atlético, Libertadores) were already there. "GUG" (Nadim's first club)
was left out of the text because the voice spelled it out.
